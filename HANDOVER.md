## How things work (merged in session 2)

### Mobile browser (merged: #39, #45)

- **Decisions (owner, 2026-11-15; the team decided to support mobile browsers):**
  mobile BROWSER only (no manifest, service worker or wrapper app); LANDSCAPE
  ONLY; steering the hook is by dragging a finger, everything else by
  on-screen buttons; tapping the canvas never means confirm (confirm also
  jumps and reels in, so stray taps would misfire). After the first phone
  test (iPhone, Chrome): touching the water should cast without CONFIRM, and
  steering the hook felt laggy.
- **How it works:** `InputCore` has a third source: `touchDown/touchUp(action)`
  (same held, edge and tap-latch rules as keys) and `setTouchAxis(x, y)` (added
  to keys/stick in `moveX/moveY`, clamped). `Device` gained `'touch'`;
  `clearKeys()` also releases touch. `src/input/touch.ts` builds a DOM overlay
  (pad, QUICK / HEAVY / BLOCK / CONFIRM, SHOP) with Pointer Events tracked per
  `pointerId`; the pad hit-tests with pointer capture so sliding changes
  direction (diagonals allowed). A finger down anywhere that is not a button:
  - **casts** if the ocean is idle (`input.tapped()`, its own signal, read only
    by the idle ocean; never `confirm`, so no jump in a fight or reel-in while
    sinking);
  - **steers the hook 1:1** while fishing: finger travel converted to game px
    (`input.dragDX()`), capped at `ocean.hook.touchSteerMaxSpeed` (1200 px/s).
    The first version used a joystick capped at `steerSpeed` (260 px/s), which
    trailed far behind a thumb and read as lag;
  - is also a small floating stick (8 px dead zone, full at 30 px) for walking
    in fights and braking or sinking faster while fishing.
  - **quick swipes** (40 px within 180 ms, `swipeDir`, only fights act on
    them): left/right dash, up jumps (one press of `up`), down crouches
    (holds `down` until the finger lifts). Rhaveen: all controls should work
    by touch, not only by buttons.
  The buttons follow the scene (`setScene`, called from `game.go`): fights show
  QUICK, HEAVY, BLOCK and DASH, with no CONFIRM or SHOP (Rhaveen: a CONFIRM
  that makes him jump "makes no sense"); fishing shows REEL IN and SHOP; the
  shop BUY and BACK; cutscene and result OK. DASH dashes the way the pad or
  drag points, or away from the fish with no direction (`input.dashDir()`;
  the fight turns it into the existing defend-plus-direction dash).
  **Walking by drag is 1.5x faster** than keys or pad
  (`fight.samurai.touchWalkScale`, owner's choice, so phones are a little
  easier); bots never set it, so the balance snapshot is unchanged.
  The phone hint text is in `text.touch` in the manifest; the device starts as
  `touch` when the overlay is on, so the hint shows before the first touch. The overlay exists only when
  `(pointer: coarse)` matches or the URL has `?touch=1`; desktop is unchanged.
  The prompt bar is hidden while `device === 'touch'`.
- **Portrait:** a full-screen CSS message covers the page, and `main.ts` skips
  steps and render while `blocked()` (resets the clock each frame, so no dt
  jump on rotating back). Pad/buttons/drag are released on rotation, blur and
  tab hide. Music keeps playing in portrait (not paused).
- **Buttons (#45):** labels sit in a span pinned to the exact centre, and
  `index.html` sets `-webkit-text-size-adjust: 100%`; iOS enlarged text in
  landscape by itself and pushed REEL IN off-centre. The right-hand buttons
  fill from the bottom-right corner, so a lone REEL IN / BUY / OK sits there.
- **Verified on an iPhone by Rhaveen** (see "Verified on real devices").
  **Not verified:** Android, iPad, multi-touch comfort on small phones.
- **Lag diagnosis:** `?debug=perf` shows the build's short commit, fps, the
  worst frame of the last second and the delay from a touch to the game step
  that used it. Ask testers to open the preview with it if anything feels slow.

### Jump, crouch and dodge-only specials (merged: #36, by a teammate)

- **Controls:** in the fight, W / up / Space (pad: A or up) jumps, S / down
  (pad: down) held crouches. No new `Action`: `up`, `confirm` and `down` are
  reused. Jump is ground-only, 120 px high, 0.7 s in the air, with air
  steering; no slashing, blocking or dashing in the air. Crouch halves the
  hurtbox (`standHeight` 120, `crouchHeight` 60 in `fight.samurai`). The
  apex stays below the top of a high shot (126 px), so high shots can only
  be ducked; a test checks every jump timing.
- **Specials** (`special` on a `FishDef`, tuning in `fight.fish.special`):
  the fish backs off to `aimDist`, flashes purple and shows JUMP! / DUCK!
  plus a danger band at the hit height, then attacks. Block and dash
  invulnerability do not help. Swordfish: low charge across the whole deck,
  then a long open window. Eel: floor shock wave (Rhaveen's electric eel idea
  from Pending 7, as a jump-over wave rather than a circle). Pufferfish: two
  spines at head height. Shark: tail-slap floor wave. Squid: ink shots high,
  low, high. Projectiles are drawn above the darkness and ink.
- Jumping above `size x clearScale` also dodges a biter's lunge.
- Striking the fish during a special's tell, charge or volley lands (it
  cannot slip then), stuns it and still costs the special its full cooldown.
- `docs/DESIGN.md` (4-button duel, "slippery fish never attack") is still
  not updated for this; it should be.
- **This reverses a team decision:** "slippery fish stay harmless"
  (Rhaveen, 2026-11-14). The eel, pufferfish and squid can now hurt the
  samurai, so their fights show his health bar. The test "slippery fish never
  attack" became "fish with no attack and no special never hurt the samurai".
  Merged by a teammate on 2026-11-15, so the team accepted it.
- No jump or crouch art exists: the poses are the walk and idle frames,
  stretched and squashed by `samuraiTransform`.
- Verified: 91 tests (new ones: jump arc, clear heights vs. tells, each

### Announcer (merged: #30, #40, #42, #43)

The fight announcer now plays **recorded clips through a Web Audio effect
chain** instead of browser speech synthesis. This replaces the flat robot
voice, which was a deliberate joke in the original design; Rhaveen found it
robotic and asked for an arcade fighting-game announcer, so this change is
for the team to accept or reject.

- **Voices (chosen by ear by Rhaveen on 2026-11-15 on a dev-only audition
  page, since removed):** every line is Kenney's "Voiceover Pack: Fighter"
  (CC0) with the `deep` treatment, except **"Fight", which is Alba
  MacKenna's own-FX recording (CC-BY 4.0, credit required, see CREDITS.md)
  with the `demonPlus` treatment**. Rhaveen chose a second voice for "Fight"
  knowingly, for more energy. "Fight" was made 3 dB quieter (#40,
  `demonPlus.gain` 4 -> 1) to sit closer to the other lines.
- **Playback (#43):** announcer clips and sound effects are decoded to Web
  Audio buffers right after the first touch or key, and effects play
  through Web Audio (no `<audio>` start-up delay on iOS); music is an
  `<audio>` element. Failed loads are retried, music retries after 3 s, and
  the audio context is resumed on every touch and key.
- **Formats (#42):** every `.ogg` has an AAC `.m4a` twin;
  `src/core/audioFormat.ts` picks `.m4a` when the browser cannot play Ogg
  (older iOS). New audio must be added in both formats:
  `afconvert -f m4af -d aac in.ogg in.m4a`.
- **Where to change it:** `voice` in `src/manifest.ts`. `clips` maps each
  line to a file; `preset` is the treatment for all lines; `linePresets`
  overrides it per line; `presets` holds the treatment numbers (pitch via
  `rate`, `drive`, `lowBoost`, `highCut`, `reverb`, `echo`, `gain`, ...).
  The effect chain is `src/core/voicefx.ts`; which clip a line uses is the
  pure `planAnnounce` in `src/core/announcer.ts`.
- **Still speech synthesis:** the fallback when a clip is missing or fails
  to load, and "Upgrade!" in the shop (no recording exists).
- **Fish names are no longer spoken** (`voice.speakFishName: false`); no
  recording can say them and the name is on screen. Set it to `true` to
  have the synthesised voice say the name after the recorded "Round N".
  Rhaveen has not given a clear answer on this, so treat it as open.
- **Rounds:** recorded "Round one" to "Round five"; from round 6 the generic
  clip says "Ready".
- **Win lines:** there is no "K.O." recording in this pack, so a win says
  "You win", a perfect win "Flawless victory", a time-out "Time", a loss
  "You lose".
- Rejected along the way: packs under CC-BY-SA (VoiceBosch), which would
  force share-alike on the game's assets; Aimee Smith's and Kenney's other
  pack (auditioned, not chosen); Wozniak and qubodup (too few lines).

### Deployment, CI and pull request previews

- **Every pull request is tested, built and published** by
  `.github/workflows/preview.yml` to the `gh-pages` branch under
  `pr-preview/pr-<number>/`, and a bot comments the play link on the PR
  (`https://yurtemre7.github.io/samurai-hook/pr-preview/pr-<number>/`). The
  folder is removed when the PR closes. This also gives every PR a test run.
  Pull requests from forks are skipped (their token is read-only).
- **GitHub Pages serves the `gh-pages` branch** (Settings, Pages, Source:
  "Deploy from a branch", `gh-pages`, `/ (root)`; switched on 2026-11-15,
  issue #21). Do not change it back to "GitHub Actions": the preview links
  and the live site both depend on it.
- **`main` is published** by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` to the root of
  `gh-pages` on every push, after the tests pass. The `pr-preview/` folder is
  left alone.
- Two third-party actions are used, pinned to major versions:
  `rossjrw/pr-preview-action@v1` and `JamesIves/github-pages-deploy-action@v4`.
- **itch.io**: `.github/workflows/itch.yml` runs on every push to `main` (and
  manually via "Run workflow"). It runs `npm ci`, `npm test`, `npm run build`
  and pushes `dist/` with itch's official `butler` CLI (downloaded from
  `broth.itch.zone`) to `yurtemre7/samurai-hook:html5`, with the user version
  `<package.json version>+<short SHA>`. Target and channel are in the
  workflow's top-level `env:`.
  - Until the `BUTLER_API_KEY` repository secret exists the workflow is
    skipped with a `::notice::` (it does not fail, so `main` stays green).
  - One-time setup, by the itch.io account owner:
    1. On itch.io go to Settings, API keys
       (https://itch.io/user/settings/api-keys) and generate a new API key.
    2. In the GitHub repo go to Settings, Secrets and variables, Actions,
       New repository secret. Name it `BUTLER_API_KEY` and paste the key.
    3. On the itch.io game's Edit page make sure "Kind of project" is HTML.
       After the first automated upload, tick "This file will be played in
       the browser" for the `html5` channel upload, and remove any old
       manually uploaded zip so players get the automated build.
    4. Re-run the workflow from the Actions tab ("Run workflow").
    5. For phones: the itch.io embed options may need "Mobile friendly"
       ticked and the orientation set to landscape. Unverified.

## Decisions

Team answers (2026-11-14):

- Fight controls: 4-button duel (move, quick slash, heavy slash, defend).
- Art: CC0 pixel art; code-drawn placeholders where no licence is confirmed.
- Roster: 8 to 10 fish; the plan has 9.
- Shop: 3 endless tracks only (damage, max depth, gold bonus).
- Audio: CC0 sound files, credited.

- Gamepad: tested with a simulated gamepad, then confirmed by a human.

Orchestrator's calls (small and reversible):

  pure and testable and there is no framework for the team to learn.
- Progress is saved in localStorage.
- Below 170 m the roster repeats in tougher, richer tiers.
- "Equipment" is the name of each damage level, not separate items.
- Losing a fight costs only the fish.
- The design doc lives at docs/DESIGN.md.
- The plan was approved on 2026-11-14, with the spoken announcer (browser
  speech synthesis, switch in `manifest.ts` under `voice`).
- `src/game/state.ts` was written by the orchestrator in the scaffold

## Known issues and open questions

- **iPhone audio (fixed, 2026-11-15):** "Round 1" and the fight music were
  silent on an iPhone because they were Ogg files (#42 added `.m4a` twins).
  Right after that merge they were silent again on the live site: the new
  files returned 404 for a few minutes while GitHub Pages published, and the
  game remembered a failed load for the whole session (#43 retries). Lesson:
  after a deploy, wait until GitHub Pages serves the new files before
  testing, and test on the real device with `?debug=perf`.
- **Process lesson (2026-11-15):** merged changes were once declared fine on
  tests and a build alone, without playing a fight on the merged `main`;
  audio regressions reached the live site. The browser tests in #44 and the
  PR checklist exist because of this.
- The first commit (plan and scaffold) went straight to `main` because an
  empty repo gives pull requests no base. Everything after it goes through
  PRs.
- The round counter and save persist in localStorage; use
  `game.state.reset()` to start over.
- `npm test` prints a large balance table; that is intended.
- Open question for the team: is the stand-in squid acceptable, or should
  it stay a placeholder until a better sprite is found?
- **W A S D in the fight (reported by Rhaveen, Chrome on a Mac), fixed:** A
  and D did not move the samurai while the arrow keys and J K L E worked.
  The fix does two things, and Rhaveen confirmed on 2026-11-15 that held A
  and D now walk. Which of the two was the cause on that machine was never
  isolated: (1) the game now claims every bound key with `preventDefault`
  (no modifiers held), as it already did for the arrows, so macOS
  press-and-hold cannot interrupt a held letter; (2) W A S D J K L E are
  also bound by the printed letter (`KeyboardEvent.key`), not only by key
  position, for non-QWERTY layouts. Also: an autorepeat key-down no longer
  latches a fresh tap, and the shop hint shows `A D` only, since up and
  down do nothing there. (The fight hint shows `W A S D` again since W / S
  jump and crouch.)
- **Key debugger:** add `?debug=keys` to the URL to see what the browser
  reports for each key event and which action it mapped to. Use it first
  for any future "key does nothing" report.
- Pull requests are tested and built by the PR preview workflow (see
  "Deployment, CI and pull request previews"); the bot's comment has the
  play link. The dedicated CI and browser-test checks are in #37 and #44.
- Scripted checks in an in-app browser pane cannot watch fights in motion
